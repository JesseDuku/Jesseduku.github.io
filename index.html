About

Most of my work looks at how floods and droughts are changing as the climate shifts, and what that means for the communities in their path. I am interested in compound hazards, cases where two risks hit at once and do more damage together than either would alone, and in extreme events more generally.

To get at this, I combine statistical hydrology, satellite observations, network theory and machine learning. So far that has meant working out why the Caspian Sea is shrinking, contributing to work on urban fire resilience, and following droughts through the global grain trade. The question that drives me is how a changing climate, together with human pressures, puts people and the systems they rely on at risk.

Earth’s Future · 2026

The Shrinking Caspian Sea

The world’s largest inland water body has been falling for three decades. We combined satellite altimetry, GRACE and long river records to work out how much of the decline comes from a warming climate and how much from less river water reaching the sea.
